> **In short:** Eventbrite dominates the market in terms of recognition, but its fees on paid tickets can absorb a significant share of your margin. There are several serious alternatives — Weezevent, Billetweb, Ticketto and others — each with specific strengths around fees, payouts, check-in and branding. This guide compares them across seven objective criteria so you can pick the one that fits your situation.

## Why look for alternatives to Eventbrite

Eventbrite is the best-known event management platform in the world, and for many organizers it's the default first choice. But "best known" doesn't always mean "best suited". There are four recurring reasons organizers go looking for alternatives.

1. **High fees on paid tickets**. Eventbrite's commercial plans apply a percentage commission plus a fixed fee on every ticket sold. For events with an average ticket above €30 and volumes over 100 attendees, the total cost can exceed 5% of revenue — which, on €50,000/year of ticket sales, means roughly €2,500 of eroded margin.
2. **Dependence on an external brand**. The Eventbrite registration page is Eventbrite-branded: your event lives under someone else's name. For anyone building a brand around recurring events, that's a positioning handicap and a missed opportunity to own the attendee relationship.
3. **Support that doesn't scale to critical cases**. Standard chat support is fine for routine questions, but payment disputes, complex refunds or processor issues are often escalated to a slower tier — frustrating when you're troubleshooting on the day of the event.
4. **Limited control over payouts and data**. With marketplace platforms, funds may be held until after the event and paid out on the platform's schedule. Direct-processor models (where money lands in your own Stripe account) give you faster access and full visibility.

None of these points disqualifies Eventbrite outright. But each one is a good reason to evaluate alternatives against your specific context.

## The 7 criteria for evaluating a platform

Before comparing names, align on the criteria. These are the seven parameters that generally weigh most heavily on your bottom line at year-end.

1. **Pricing model**: flat fee, per-ticket commission, or hybrid?
2. **Who pays the fee**: only the organizer, or is a service fee passed on to the attendee at checkout?
3. **Payment processing**: direct Stripe (transparent fees of roughly 1.5% in the EU) or an aggregator that adds its own margin on top?
4. **Payouts**: money in your own account in real time, or held by the platform until after the event?
5. **Check-in**: does it require dedicated hardware, or is your staff's smartphone enough?
6. **White-label and branding**: is the registration page yours, or does it carry the platform's logo and marketplace links?
7. **Bulk import, API and support**: can you upload 2,000 attendees from a CSV in one go, integrate with your CRM, and reach real support on event day?

## The alternatives (plus Eventbrite as a benchmark)

### 1. Weezevent

**Origin**: France (Paris) · **Target**: medium-to-large events, festivals, trade fairs

Weezevent is a leading European platform for festivals and recurring events. It offers online ticketing, check-in, cashless/RFID and access control in a single platform. Pricing is mainly **commission-based**, typically in the 2–5% range depending on volume and the features you enable, and negotiable for enterprise contracts.

**Strengths**: a mature solution for festivals and concerts, customizable branding, EU data hosting, multilingual.

**Limits**: overkill for events under 500 attendees, complex setup for simple cases, non-trivial fees.

**Perfect for**: music festivals, B2B trade fairs, multi-day events with granular access control.

### 2. Billetweb

**Origin**: France · **Target**: fee-conscious organizers, micro-events

Billetweb is a no-frills European answer to Eventbrite, positioned on **aggressive pricing** — typically around 1% on tickets sold, with free events at zero cost. A bare-bones interface and solid basic features.

**Strengths**: among the lowest fees on the market, a brandable public page, GDPR compliance with EU data.

**Limits**: a less polished UX, a reduced integrations ecosystem, and advanced features (time slots, cashless, deep APIs) that are missing or basic.

**Perfect for**: organizers with low volumes who want to minimize fees without needing sophisticated features.

### 3. Ticketto

**Origin**: EU · **Target**: organizers who want smartphone check-in and transparent, predictable fees

A note on transparency: Ticketto is the platform we build. I'm saying so openly because the rest of this section is verifiable facts, not marketing. Ticketto uses a **flat-fee** model (Free, Pro €29/month, Business €79/month) with **zero platform fees** — you only pay the standard Stripe transaction fee (~1.5% in the EU), and the money lands directly in your own Stripe account. Check-in is exclusively via smartphone, with no dedicated scanners. Time slots, bulk import, automated communications and a real-time dashboard are included. Data is hosted in the EU, a DPA is available.

**Strengths**: transparent pricing with no surprises, scalable smartphone check-in (as many stations as you have staff devices), direct Stripe integration with instant payouts, fully white-label registration pages.

**Limits**: not aimed at the ultra-large festival segment with dedicated RFID (we don't compete with Weezevent on 10,000+ person events), and a third-party plugin ecosystem still being built.

**Perfect for**: courses and training, corporate conventions, small-to-medium cultural events — anything between 50 and 3,000 attendees.

### 4. Ticket Tailor

**Origin**: UK · **Target**: organizers who want flat per-ticket pricing and no service fees on attendees

Ticket Tailor is a popular international alternative built around a **flat fee per ticket sold** rather than a percentage of revenue, which is attractive for higher-priced tickets. It connects to your own Stripe or PayPal account, so payouts are direct.

**Strengths**: predictable per-ticket cost regardless of ticket price, direct payouts, clean and simple interface, no mandatory booking fee passed to attendees.

**Limits**: fewer marketing/discovery features than Eventbrite, lighter on advanced access control and cashless.

**Perfect for**: workshops, conferences and paid events with mid-to-high ticket prices where a percentage fee would hurt.

### 5. Hi.Events / open-source self-hosting

**Origin**: open-source · **Target**: technical teams that want full control

For organizers with technical resources, self-hosted and open-source ticketing tools let you run the whole stack yourself, paying only your own Stripe fees and hosting. This maximizes control over data and branding at the cost of maintenance effort.

**Strengths**: lowest possible variable cost, full data ownership, complete branding control.

**Limits**: you own setup, hosting, updates and support; no marketplace discovery; requires in-house technical capability.

**Perfect for**: organizations with developer resources and recurring events that justify the maintenance overhead.

### 6. Eventbrite (benchmark)

**Origin**: USA · **Target**: any type of event, broad audience

Eventbrite is the market benchmark for recognition and user base. It offers a Free plan for free events and commercial plans for paid events with percentage fees plus a fixed fee, and a rich ecosystem of integrations.

**Strengths**: brand awareness and potential organic traffic from the Eventbrite marketplace itself, a marketing ecosystem, and a consumer app installed by millions.

**Limits**: significant fees on paid tickets, US data under SCC for EU organizers, platform branding visible to the attendee, and payouts that may be held until after the event.

**Suited for**: organizers who want to tap into the organic visibility of the Eventbrite marketplace and are willing to pay the fee premium for that benefit.

## How to choose based on your situation

Instead of an absolute ranking (which would be misleading — every platform is better in a specific context), here's a simple decision frame.

**Are you organizing a festival or an event with RFID/cashless?**
→ Weezevent is the first choice. Eventbrite is a fallback; most lean platforms aren't sized for this case.

**Are you organizing training events, courses or corporate conventions of 50–3,000 attendees?**
→ Ticketto (a flat fee that already pays off around €25K/year in ticket sales) or Ticket Tailor (a flat per-ticket fee if your volumes are very irregular).

**Do you sell few tickets a year (<500 total) and just want the cheapest tool?**
→ Billetweb or Eventbrite's Free plan (free events only).

**Do you have developer resources and want maximum control over data and branding?**
→ A self-hosted/open-source option.

**Do you want to leverage the Eventbrite marketplace to draw traffic to your event page?**
→ Eventbrite, but budget the fee as a user-acquisition cost.

One last tip: before committing to any platform, run a **small pilot event** (even a free one, with 30 attendees). Test the entire flow — from registration to check-in to reporting — before you stake your biggest event on it. That half day saves you from surprises you'd otherwise pay dearly for.

## Conclusions

None of these alternatives to Eventbrite is "universally better". Each wins in a specific context. The right question isn't "which is the best alternative?", but "which is best for my type of event, my volume, my economic model?".

If your profile is an **organizer of small-to-medium, training or corporate events, with volumes above €20K/year and zero desire to manage dedicated hardware**, Ticketto is probably the choice that saves you the most margin.
